# Tramp
Authors: Jonas, Marcus

## Summary

> Each night, a player receive a Potion of Panic. Veiled PoP:s might make the holder drunk. If you were "mad" about being an Outsider, you keep your ability while dead.

*Bugrit, millennium hand an' shrimp...*

The Tramp causes misinformation via making players drunk, and is a source of Potion of Panic on the grim from the good team.

## How to run
Each night, for as long as the Tramp has their ability, choose a living player that is not holding a Potion of Panic and put a Potion of Panic on that player.

While alive, monitor the Tramp's behaviour and if you are convinced that they are trying to convey to other players that they are any kind of Outsider, put the **MESSY** reminder token on the Tramp and keep it active even if they die.

## Examples
On the first night, the Storyteller chose Tina.
Tina is now holding a Potion of Panic.
As that Potion of Panic is Veiled, the Storyteller may now put the Drunk reminder token on Tina.
If the Potion of Panic is Unveiled, the Drunk reminder token is removed.

Lars the Tramp is being questioned by another player of what their role is.
Lars won't give any clear answer and just dodges the question.
The Storyteller may at this point judge that Lars is trying to convey that they are the Tramp and put the **MESSY** reminder token on them.

Richard the Tramp is executed.
For the entire game they have been adamant that they are the Druid.
The Storyteller should NOT put the **MESSY** reminder token on them and the Tramp will become unactive.

## Tips & Tricks

- Each night you place a Potion of Panic on a player not already holding one, and while it stays veiled it might make that player drunk and feed misinformation.
- To keep your ability after you die, be openly "mad" about being an Outsider - act the part of the Tramp rather than hiding behind a Townsfolk claim.
- Claiming a Townsfolk role instead means you lose your ability the moment you die, so decide early whether a death-proof ability is worth outing yourself.
- A veiled Potion of Panic shows the same backside as a good potion in the Cauldron, so no one can tell your Panic apart from a genuine good potion until it is unveiled.

## Bluffing as the Tramp

- The Tramp places a Potion of Panic each night, but those only show as Panic once unveiled - while veiled they look like good potions, so your claimed handouts are hard for the town to check.
- Evil teammates like the Aberration and Megalomaniac also put Potion of Panic into play, giving you cover to claim their handouts as your own.
- The Tramp rewards looking mad about being an Outsider, so it is a great bluff for excusing evasive or strange behaviour you were going to show anyway.
- Claim that one of your Panics drunked a player to explain away a piece of information that does not add up.
- Leaning into an obvious Outsider act also steers the town away from suspecting you of a more dangerous evil role. This is especially valuable while your Megalomaniac or Aberration is still alive, since the town blames you, a harmless Outsider, for the Potions of Panic instead of hunting the real source.
- Claim a vague Outsider while you are alive, then once you die back into the specific Tramp claim to account for the Potions of Panic that have been appearing.
- As that dead Tramp, tell the town you no longer hand out Potion of Panic so the Panic source looks dealt with, even while your Aberration or Megalomaniac keeps quietly producing more.
- If you are the Aberration yourself, do not commit to the Outsider bluff while alive: a dead Aberration stops giving Potion of Panic, so keep it in reserve and, only once you die, reveal you were a Tramp in disguise, since the Panics drying up matches a non-mad Tramp that lost its ability on death.

## Fighting the Tramp

The Clofou can secretly turn an Outsider evil, so a Tramp you thought was good may now be working for evil. This section covers how the good team can play against a hidden Tramp.

- A turned Tramp keeps handing out Potion of Panic every night, now deliberately feeding its team's Aberration, so watch who keeps receiving Panic and why.
- Re-examine Panics you had written off as harmless good-Tramp play, since after the turn each one can be enabling an evil effect.
- A Tramp that suddenly acts oddly or against the town can point you toward the Clofou, since it must have neighboured or traded with the demon to be turned.
- Since a real Tramp is rewarded for looking like an Outsider, do not read that Outsider act alone as proof it is still good.
