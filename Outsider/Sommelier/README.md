# Sommelier
Authors: Jonas, Marcus

## Summary

> Each night one player who would receive a potion might receive one of the opposite alignment instead.

*The discovery of a wine is of greater moment than the discovery of a constellation. The universe is too full of stars.*

The Sommelier acts as a negative force upon the distribution of Potions in the game, causing confusion and increasing the amount of harmful effects.

## How to run
Anytime a player would receive a potion and the Sommelier did not already trigger this night decide if the receiving player should receive a potion of the opposite alignment instead.
If you do, choose any Potion of the opposite alignment that they do not yet hold and put it on them.
If a person for any reason cannot receive a potion of the opposite alignment, this ability cannot trigger upon them.

Potion of Panic is a "special" alignment potion, and therefor has no opposite. It cannot be replaced by the Sommelier.

## Examples
Marcus the Distiller chooses Jonas the Druid.
The Distiller said that Jonas should receive a good Potion but the Sommelier is used to change that potion to an evil one (e.g. Potion of Apathy).

Jonas the Chemist chooses 1. And then chooses Marcus's Potion of Conviction and Sandra's Potion of Panic to be traded.
Marcus receives the Potion of Panic but the Sommelier is used to change the Potion of Conviction to an evil potion (e.g. Potion of Terror).
Any subsequent Potions that are received cannot be changed by the Sommelier this night.

## Tips & Tricks

- Your ability is out of your hands: once a night the Storyteller may flip the alignment of one potion a player was about to receive, and you make no choice and learn nothing.
- It usually turns good potions evil, so a potion in play may not be the alignment its source intended.
- It can also flip the other way and turn an evil potion into a good one, though as an Outsider it more often harms the town than helps it.
- Potion of Panic has no opposite alignment, so it can never be flipped by your ability.
- A player cannot be given a duplicate potion type, so if their only opposite-alignment option is one they already hold, your ability cannot trigger on them.
- With no night action and no information, you confirm yourself by reasoning: point to a potion that arrived flipped from what a good source openly promised.

## Bluffing as the Sommelier

- Claiming a role that actively harms the town reads as honest, since few good players would invent a self-sabotaging Outsider, so the Sommelier makes your cover look genuine.
- It is a ready excuse for good potions turning evil, so you can blame any soured potion on your Sommelier ability whether or not it really happened.
- Claiming it also lets you wave away why beneficial potions you were "supposed" to hand out arrived harmful instead.
- There is nothing to prove night to night, so the town cannot catch you out on missing information.

## Fighting the Sommelier

The Clofou can secretly turn an Outsider evil, so a Sommelier you thought was good may now be working for evil. This section covers how the good team can play against a hidden Sommelier.

- A turned Sommelier plays much like before, since its ability already tends to harm the town by souring potions, but it now also wins with evil and votes against you.
- Its ability is Storyteller-controlled, so turning evil grants it no new powers; treat it as an ordinary hidden evil player and judge it on behaviour and votes.
- A Sommelier that suddenly acts oddly or against the town can point you toward the Clofou, since it must have neighboured or traded with the demon to be turned.
- Because a Sommelier already muddies potion alignments, do not read soured potions alone as proof of who has turned.
