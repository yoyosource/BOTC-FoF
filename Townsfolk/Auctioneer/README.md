# Auctioneer
Authors: Jonas, Marcus

## Summary

> Each night*, learn if an evil player traded tonight.

*Aaaaaaaand, sold to the robed stranger with the pitchfork!*

The Auctioneer encourages trading within the town, utilizing the mechanic to gain information on the alignment of the other players.

## How to run
Wake up the Auctioneer.
Show them yes if any evil player started the night with a 'Trade X' token.
Show them no otherwise.
Put them back to sleep.

## Examples
On the 1st day, 3 pairs of players announces that they wish to trade potions.
The Storyteller puts a 'Trade X' reminder token on each of those players.
The following night, Luna the Auctioneer wakes up.
Since 1 of the Reminder tokens is on the John the Aberration, an evil player, the Storyteller signals a Yes to the Auctioneer.

On day 2nd day, A pair of players announces that they wish to trade potions.
The Storyteller put a 'Trade X' reminder token on each of those players.
The following night, Chris the Auctioneer wakes up.
Since neither Reminder token is on an evil player, the Auctioneer learns a No.

## Tips & Tricks

- You wake from the second night on and learn a single yes or no: whether any evil player had a trade happening that night.
- A yes means at least one of the players trading that night is evil, so cross-reference exactly who traded to narrow your suspects.
- A no clears everyone who traded that night of being evil, so it is a strong way to confirm a group of good players in one go.
- Drum up bidders by encouraging trades; the more players trade on a night, the more your single bit of information is worth - a no vouches for a big group, a yes flags a smaller set.
- Your result can be wrong if you were drunk or poisoned, or if a Potion of Stress makes a trader misregister their alignment to you.

## Bluffing as the Auctioneer

- The Auctioneer only produces a yes or no each night, so it is a low-detail claim that is easy to sustain.
- Time your claimed results to protect your team: say no on nights your side traded to clear them, or yes to throw suspicion onto a good trading pair.
- Alternatively, throw an evil teammate under the bus with a yes that points at them, spending their cover to cement your own credibility as a trustworthy Auctioneer.
- Keep every claim consistent with the public trade record, since your info hinges entirely on who actually traded and contradictions are easy to spot.
- You wake from the second night, so you have no first-night result - do not invent one.
- Being on the evil team, you already know when your side traded, which makes it easy to give believable results.
